# Make the Next Step Visible

## 原文

A long task can feel difficult even when the work itself is not very hard. Often, the real problem is that the next action is unclear. A note such as “work on the report” sounds useful, but it does not tell you what to do first. When you return to the task, you must spend time deciding where to begin.

One simple habit is to stop each work period by writing down the next small action. It should be clear enough that you can start without making another decision. Instead of “continue the report,” write “compare the June and July sales numbers” or “write two sentences about the main result.” This gives your future self a clear entry point.

This habit is especially useful when you need to switch between tasks. After a meeting or a break, it can take several minutes to remember what you were doing. A short note reduces this restart time. It also helps at the end of the day. You can leave work knowing that tomorrow’s first step is already waiting for you.

The note does not need to describe the whole plan. In fact, too much detail may become another form of delay. Its job is only to make the next action visible and easy to begin.

Before you finish today, try leaving one clear instruction for yourself. Put it at the top of your document or task list. Tomorrow, notice whether you begin more quickly. A small note may not complete the work, but it can protect your attention and keep the task moving.
