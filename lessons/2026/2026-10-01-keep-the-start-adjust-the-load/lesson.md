# Keep the Start, Adjust the Load

## 原文

Some days you sit down to study and the words come easily. Other days, the same page feels heavy, and your mind keeps drifting away. The difference is often not motivation but energy. Energy changes from day to day, and a learning plan that ignores this will slowly break.

Many learners make one of two mistakes. The first is pushing hard every single day, treating a full lesson as non-negotiable. This works for a week or two, and then one tired evening makes the whole plan feel impossible. The second mistake is the opposite: when energy is low, they skip the lesson completely and promise to do double tomorrow. Tomorrow rarely cooperates.

There is a third way: separate what stays fixed from what can change. First, keep a fixed starting time — for example, twenty minutes after dinner, every day. The time itself becomes a signal. When the clock reaches that point, learning begins, and no new decision is needed. Second, keep one core task that never disappears, even on the worst day. It might be one page of reading or five new words. It is small, but it keeps the habit alive.

Everything else can change size. Match the intensity to your energy. On a strong day, read two articles, write a paragraph, and review old vocabulary. On a weak day, scale the lesson down to its core: read a few paragraphs slowly, then stop. You have not failed; you have adjusted.

This approach protects the most important thing, which is showing up. A learner who shows up anyway on tired days keeps the rhythm alive, and rhythm carries a plan across the busy months. The amount you do on one single day matters less than the number of days you begin.
