# Protecting a Block of Focused Time

## 原文

In a busy workday, many people move quickly from one request to another. A message arrives, a meeting begins, or a colleague needs a quick answer. Responding to these things is sometimes necessary. The problem starts when every request gets the same immediate attention. By the end of the day, you may have been busy for hours but made little progress on the work that needs careful thought.

One practical solution is to protect a short block of focused time. It does not have to be a whole morning. Even twenty-five minutes can be useful if you choose one clear task before you begin. Close unrelated tabs, silence non-urgent notifications, and put the task in front of you. The goal is not to finish everything. It is to give one important piece of work enough uninterrupted attention to move forward.

This habit also requires a simple way to handle interruptions. Before you switch to another task, record what you have just completed and what you plan to do next. For example, you might write, “Checked the first section; next, compare the two examples.” When you return to the original task, that note saves you from asking the same question again: “Where was I?” It creates a clear path back into the work.

You do not need to ignore people or refuse every request. Instead, decide what can wait for a short time and answer it later. If something is truly urgent, handle it and then return to the original task with your note. Over time, this small boundary can make your day feel less scattered. You may still have many tasks, but you will have protected time for the work that deserves your best attention.
