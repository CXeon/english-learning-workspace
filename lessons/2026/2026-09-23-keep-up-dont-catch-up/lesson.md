# Keep Up, Don't Catch Up

## 原文

A busy season at work or at home can put your learning plan under pressure. Meetings run long, tasks pile up, and slowly you fall behind on your plan. You miss a lesson here, a review there. When a free evening finally appears, a question comes with it: should you go back and complete everything you missed, or should you simply continue with today's lesson?

Many learners choose the first option. They want to catch up on every missed lesson before moving on. It sounds responsible, but it rarely works. The pile of missed content grows faster than your free time, and every catch-up session feels like paying a debt. Learning becomes heavy again — and heavy learning is the first thing a busy person drops.

A healthier rule is the opposite: keep up with the current plan, and let the missed content wait. Do today's lesson, even if it is a lighter version of it — ten minutes of reading instead of a full session. This keeps the plan alive and keeps you in touch with the material. Then, if extra energy appears, pick the one missed lesson that matters most, and come back to it later.

This rule works because order matters less than continuity. If you finish lesson nine before lesson seven, nothing breaks. The words in lesson seven will still make sense next week. But if the plan stops completely, restarting it takes real effort.

So let go of the idea that you must complete every lesson in order. Falling behind is normal; giving up is the only real failure. Keep a small daily connection to your plan, and the busy season will pass while your learning continues.
