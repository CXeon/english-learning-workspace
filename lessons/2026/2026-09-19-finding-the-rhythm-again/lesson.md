# Finding the Rhythm Again

## 原文

Life gets busy. A heavy week at work, a trip, or a family matter can interrupt your learning routine. You miss one day, then two. Soon a week or more has passed, and the habit that felt natural now feels far away. Many learners know this moment well — and many make it harder than it needs to be.

The first mistake is trying to make up for lost time. You plan a long session to cover everything you missed. But a double lesson feels heavy, and heavy tasks are easy to postpone. The next day, you are busy again, and the plan quietly dies. Missed practice cannot really be recovered; it can only be replaced by today's practice.

A better approach is to pick up where you left off. Open the exact material you were using before the break. Do not start a new plan or redesign everything. The familiar text gives you a clear entry point: you already know its topic, and your old notes still make sense.

Then set a small daily minimum. This might be ten minutes of reading or five new words — something so small that even a busy day cannot block it. The purpose of the minimum is not fast progress. It is to rebuild the rhythm. When you study a little every day, learning starts to feel normal again, and the desire to do more returns on its own.

Finally, protect the progress you have already made. The days you missed did not erase your earlier work. Your vocabulary is still there; it just needs to be used again.

If you have been away from learning for a while, start small, start today, and keep it simple. Momentum returns quickly once you begin.
