# Returning After an Interruption

## 原文

Interruptions are a normal part of work. A message arrives, a coworker asks a question, or you need to join a meeting. The interruption may last only a few minutes, but returning to the original task can take much longer. You may remember the general goal but forget the exact point where you stopped.

A small “restart note” can make this easier. Before you switch away, write one or two short lines. Record what you just finished, what you planned to do next, and any question still in your mind. For example: “Checked the first three numbers. Next, compare the final two. Need to confirm Friday’s total.” This note takes less than a minute, but it saves you from studying the whole task again.

When you return, read the note before opening messages or checking something else. Then do the next action written there. You do not need to understand the entire project again. You only need a clear way back into the work.

Of course, you cannot prepare for every interruption. Sometimes a call or urgent request gives you no warning. In that case, create the restart note as soon as the interruption ends. Write down what you still remember, then choose one small action that will help you find your place.

This habit does not remove interruptions, but it reduces the time they take from you. More importantly, it lowers the effort needed to begin again. With a clear note, you can return your attention to the task instead of spending energy trying to remember where you were.
